
# You Are Not the Only One Watching Your TV

*How smart TVs fingerprint everything on your screen, and why you can’t simply opt-out.*

**Original Publication:** [Substack – You Are Not the Only One Watching](https://nivedithajayakumar.substack.com/p/you-are-not-the-only-one-watching)

---

When you watch a movie on Netflix or Prime Video and it suggests similar content the next day, you don’t think much of it, right? Netflix obviously logged your activity, saw what you liked, and recommended similar content to keep you on the platform longer.

That’s how most streaming services, social media apps, and e-commerce websites work. They track your activity inside their apps to get to know you better. As humans in 2026, we’ve pretty much accepted that when we go online, we’re being tracked. But what if they’re tracking us offline, too?

### Allow Me to Introduce ACR (Automatic Content Recognition)

ACR is a piece of software embedded directly into a smart TV’s operating system. ACR takes continuous snapshots of whatever is on your screen, converts them into digital "fingerprints," and sends them to a server endpoint. There, the data is matched against a massive database to identify exactly what you’re watching, down to the second.

You might assume ACR is just tracking your activity across streaming apps to do what Netflix already does. But you’ll be surprised to find out that ACR is tracking almost everything except your activity on those streaming platforms.

In a recent study conducted by researchers at Northeastern University on [ACR privacy](https://watchingthewatchers.github.io/watchingthewatchers/), they found that smart TVs collect your data and log your activity while you’re watching antenna and broadcast TV, or even when you connect another device to your TV through HDMI. Some TVs even transmit tracking data while sitting completely idle on the home screen.

### This Is Concerning for a Number of Reasons

First, most TV makers bury this feature under misleading terms like "Viewing Information Services" or "Interactive TV Features," hiding it inside huge blocks of fine print that nobody reads before clicking "Agree."

Second, they aren’t just recording what you see; they’re also recording what you hear. Researchers observed that some TVs continue sending data even when a black screen is playing with only audio.

It turns out the main time smart TVs don’t track your screen via ACR is when you’re using third-party streaming apps like Netflix or YouTube. These platforms have agreements ensuring TV ACR software won’t capture their stream, mostly because they want to track you themselves.

### So Who Are They Sending This Data To, and What Are They Doing With It?

Well, the short answer is targeted ads.

TV manufacturers collect and sell your viewing data to third-party ad networks. Then these ad networks work around the clock to send targeted ads your way.

The data tracked also contains your TV’s IP address, mapping it to the other devices on your home network. So it’s possible that watching a basketball game on your TV could mean seeing sports betting app ads pop up on your phone minutes later.

And the worst part? Some TVs transmit this ACR data completely unencrypted. On certain platforms (like VIDAA OS), audio fingerprints, permanent device IDs, and content genres are sent across the network in plain text for anyone to read.

If you’re thinking, "Well, it’s just data about what I’m watching. How much harm could it do?", consider two things:

> What you do inside your house should be nobody’s business but yours.

They aren’t just tracking if you watch the news or sports. ACR also tracks whatever is projected onto your TV screen through HDMI. If you plug your laptop into your TV to display a work meeting, a presentation, or personal photos, ACR can take snapshots of that, too.

### So Is There No Escape?

The solution can’t be to give up watching TV entirely. Live TV is already struggling as it is!

The good news is that for some platforms like Google TV, Fire TV, LG’s webOS, and VIDAA, turning off ACR in the settings actually works and stops the tracking.

But if your smart TV is one of those that doesn’t respect your privacy (like Samsung or Vizio), the researchers recommend disconnecting the TV from the internet entirely and using an external streaming box (like an Apple TV or dedicated streaming stick) that doesn’t run TV-level ACR.

### The Takeaway

We’ve spent years learning to be careful with our phones. We cover our webcams and question every app that asks for our location. Meanwhile, the biggest screen in the house sat in the living room, quietly taking notes.

So before your next movie night, spend two minutes in your TV’s settings. Hunt down the ACR option, switch it off, and if your TV won’t take no for an answer, cut its internet and plug in a streaming stick.

**The show should be on the screen, not the person watching it.**
